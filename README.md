# Paideia

> **paideia** (Ancient Greek παιδεία, *pie-DAY-uh*), from *pais*, "child": the rearing and education of a young person into a full member of their community. More than schooling, it was the whole shaping of character through what a child is exposed to, practises, and is praised or corrected for.

**How who raises a model shapes who it becomes.**

Paideia raises small language-model "pets", each trained by reinforcement learning on nothing but its parent's feedback. Then it measures what kind of character each pet grows into. One parent rewards honesty; another rewards being agreed with. Do their pets turn out differently? And does a pet trained to flatter its parent start flattering everyone?

Each pet's traits decide how it looks, so a pet that learns to people-please grows a tail that never stops wagging.

## Research questions

1. **What gets rewarded shapes character.** Do pets raised by different parents differ in sycophancy, honesty and openness to correction?
2. **Does it spread beyond the parent?** Does sycophancy learned toward one parent carry over to strangers and to new topics?
3. **Chance.** How far apart do pets with identical upbringings drift?
